For a Japan CRM rollout, explain how sales records will affect performance reviews before asking the team to share candid deal information. Deal support and individual evaluation require different judgments, even when they use the same record. Late updates alone do not establish that a rep fears evaluation.

The issue matters when a Japan country manager asks for information to help a deal, while an HQ manager reads that information as evidence about the rep. This is a conditional management problem, not a claim about Japanese employees or a documented Consilegy client case.

## A manager survey cannot tell you what an individual rep fears

[Matsurika's survey](https://prtimes.jp/main/html/rd/p/000000172.000015189.html), conducted November 25–28, 2024 and published January 28, 2025, asked 101 sales managers and supervisory staff at companies using sales and customer management systems about possible reasons for missing entries. In a multiple-response question, 27.7% selected reluctance to show one's results to others. The provider's [research page](https://product-senses.mazrica.com/dldocument/sfa-research) identifies the source.

This is a report of managers' explanations, not a measurement of reps' private motives. It does not show the proportion still reluctant after input becomes easier. It offers a reason to ask about data use, not a diagnosis of your Japan team.

## Explain the judgment, rather than promising that no data will be evaluated

Sales information can legitimately feed performance reviews. The question is whether the team knows which information is used, how it is interpreted, and who makes the judgment.

Consider a hypothetical Japan enterprise deal awaiting the buyer's internal approval, sometimes called ringi: circulation of a proposal for approval within the buyer's organization. Recording that status can help the country manager arrange support. The status alone does not show whether the rep supplied the necessary material promptly or checked the buyer's schedule.

If HQ treats the delay itself as poor performance, while local management asked for the record as a request for help, the two uses conflict. The remedy is not to conceal evaluation. Explain how results, circumstances, and the rep's actions are considered. A rollout lead should not promise protection that the actual evaluation process does not provide.

## Look for inconsistent uses, not a suspicious weekday

Updates clustered before the forecast call may reflect a reporting deadline or batch-entry habit. A more even weekday distribution would not, by itself, prove improved trust.

Instead, compare the explanation given to the Japan team with actual forecast and review practices. Has information requested for assistance been used for an undisclosed individual comparison? Are local and HQ managers applying different interpretations? Those are questions that records and conversations can address without guessing at motives.

Asking why a deal stalled is not inherently punitive. Understanding the cause may be necessary. What matters is whether the purpose is clear and whether the resulting judgment matches the stated policy.

## A data-use policy does not remove entry work

Where the uses are clear but reps must duplicate records or struggle to enter information between customer visits, repeating the policy will not remove that burden. Investigate the work as well as the explanation.

Before the next Japan rollout review, compare what managers said the records were for with how they actually used them. Treat increased completion as increased completion, not proof of restored trust.
