Ten at night. A teacher finishes the last lesson, opens her phone and finds fourteen unread chat messages. A trial-lesson request. Next week's absence. A question about the direct-debit date. A parent asking how the lesson went today. She answers what she can, leaves three for tomorrow, and goes to sleep. In the morning she cannot remember who one of the three was from.

In Japan, almost every inquiry a school receives comes through LINE, the chat app everyone uses. And LINE lives on the teacher's personal phone. There is no place where the school, as an organization, can see what has been said to a given student.

## The loss is not a late reply. It is not knowing whether you replied

A reply that arrives a day late is rarely a disaster. The problem is that whether it was sent at all exists only in one person's memory.

Miss a trial request and that family goes to the school down the street. Miss an absence notice and the seat stays empty when it could have been a make-up lesson. Leave a fee question unanswered and next month's payment fails. Each one is a small slip that "we were busy" explains away. With thirty inquiries a month, some of them will slip, every month.

And when a teacher leaves, their chat history leaves with them. The conversation with each student disappears on their last day.

## In Revenue CRM, each student's chat history belongs to the school

The school edition of Revenue CRM connects to your LINE Official Account. From the day you connect, messages students send arrive in that student's record, under "LINE history". They are kept as the school's record, not on a phone.

You reply from the same place. Open "LINE history" on a student and you see the conversation as the student sees it on top, and a send box below. Templates are ready for the common cases, such as acknowledging an absence, sending trial details, or a fee reminder. Press one, it fills the message, and you can edit it before sending. Replies sent from here stay in the history too, so "did I answer?" is no longer a memory question.

The Home screen you open first thing in the morning has a "Needs attention" list. When a student has replied on LINE, it shows as one line: "LINE reply from [name]". Instead of reading fourteen unread messages top to bottom, you start with the ones that need an answer.

One thing to be clear about. Because of how the LINE platform works, only two kinds of messages reach the CRM: what students send after you connect, and what you send from the CRM. Conversations from before the connection, and replies typed in the LINE app itself, do not come in. So after connecting, replies have to move to the CRM's send box. Skip that change and you end up with half a history.

## The first step

Once the LINE Official Account is connected, spend one week sending every reply from the CRM's send box. Rewrite just three templates in your own words (absence, trial details, fee reminder) and it becomes faster than replying from the phone. After a week, look at "Needs attention" on Home. If nothing has been left unanswered, that is your school's new normal.

The demo environment lets you try the history and the send box without sending anything.
