Japan pipeline is soft, so the alignment meeting moves from monthly to weekly. Same attendees, same dashboard, same two slides.

A quarter later the handover count is unchanged and the region has spent an extra eight hours in a call at 9pm local time.

Frequency was not the constraint. The dashboard was.

## Your rollup has no field for the part of a Japanese deal that decides it

A global alignment view is built from events. Form filled, MQL created, meeting held, stage advanced. Regions get compared on the rate at which events occur.

In Japan the decisive stretches are the ones where no event occurs. In a survey by IDEATECH and Hiroyasu Kitagawa of Demagen Research (published 16 April 2026, fielded 23 to 26 March 2026, n=307, restricted to people who took part with at least one colleague in a Japanese purchase of business software worth 3 million yen or more, and run as survey-as-PR by a company whose business is survey-as-PR, so read it for order of magnitude), more than 80% of those deals passed through two or more approval layers, and 63.6% ran 3 to 8 months. By the time a vendor was properly engaged, the buying process was already about 40% complete.

Six weeks of a champion circulating a draft internally produces exactly nothing your dashboard can render. Meeting more often does not make an absence visible.

## Both teams are complaining about something only the other one can see

This is not unique to Japan, but the Japan team feels it harder because the rep is the only person with any visibility into the silent stretch.

A survey of the two functions in Japan by Softbrain (fielded 4 to 6 October 2024, published 17 December 2024, 159 sales and 139 marketing respondents for a total of 298, conducted by IDEATECH; a vendor survey with roughly 150 per side, so treat it as ranking rather than a precise rate) asked each side the same question. Marketing's top complaint about sales was that follow-up is never improved, at 47.5%. Sales' top complaint about marketing was that lead information is insufficient, at 42.1%.

Neither of those is checkable from a rollup. Whether the information on a lead was sufficient is a property of one record. So is what the rep sent and what came back.

## Open one live deal that neither side can explain alone

Take the first fifteen minutes. Put two deals on screen that are still open.

Marketing walks the path in: what the person read, what they downloaded, what state the record was in at handover. Sales continues it: what was sent, what came back, where it is sitting now. One record, in order, in front of both.

For the first several sessions you will not reach tactics. What surfaces is definitional. The state marketing calls a qualified opportunity is not the state sales calls one, and both regional numbers were internally correct the whole time. That mismatch is invisible in aggregate and obvious in a single record.

Headquarters gets something out of this too. The reason a Japanese deal is quiet becomes narratable: who is assembling the internal case, which approval layer it is on, what document is missing. That is a far better input to a forecast than a stage name.

## Cadence follows from whether the deal is still open when you meet

Monthly, the deals available to open are mostly the ones that already closed. Reviewing a closed deal is a post-mortem, and post-mortems change the next deal at best.

The test for cadence is whether you can still open something whose outcome is not yet fixed. In Japan that test is easy to pass, given evaluation periods of 3 to 8 months. Deals survive the gap between meetings comfortably.

So weekly is not about speed. It is that at one meeting a month, two records fill the entire agenda.

**Change what the meeting opens, not how often it happens. One live deal neither team can explain on its own is worth more than a quarter of rollups.**

## Common mistakes

- **Reading a quiet Japanese deal as a stalled one.** Silence in the CRM is the normal shape of an internal approval cycle, not evidence the deal died. The distinction matters for [explaining to headquarters why Japanese deals take longer](/en/articles/why-japan-deals-take-longer/).
- **Adding the cadence without changing the unit.** More frequent rollups produce more frequent defences of each function's own number, which hardens the split rather than closing it.
- **Letting the Japan team present, rather than open.** A prepared summary from the region is another rollup. The value is in looking at an unprepared record together.
