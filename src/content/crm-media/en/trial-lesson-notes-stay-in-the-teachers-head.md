The first ten minutes of a trial lesson are the richest conversation you will ever have with that student. Why did you decide to start? What have you tried before? Where do you want to be, and by when? People are surprisingly candid with a teacher they have just met. With children, the parent sitting beside them often opens up too: "Actually, something happened at school."

Those ten minutes go into the head of whichever teacher ran the trial. Even schools with a paper intake sheet end up with a name, a phone number and an empty "goals" box. When the student enrolls and a different teacher takes over, the first lesson starts with the same questions. The student thinks "I told you this at the trial" and says nothing.

## The loss is not a missing note. It is asking the same question twice

When the trial conversation is not kept, two things happen.

First, the first real lesson becomes a second first meeting. The teacher does not know the goal or the level, so the first month is spent finding out. Most students who quit do so within three months of enrolling. Spending one of those three months on reconnaissance is expensive.

Second, you never learn why the people who did not enroll walked away. Was it the price, the schedule, or "let me think about it"? A month later nobody remembers. This is why schools with plenty of trial requests and flat enrollment cannot say what is wrong.

## In Revenue CRM, paste the trial transcript and the intake sheet fills itself

The school edition has an intake interview screen for each student, opened from "Interview" on the student record. It has six sections: how they found you, basic information, current level, goals, what went well in the trial, and what was hard in the trial.

You do not type these six sections after the lesson. At the top of the screen is "Fill the fields from a transcript". Paste the trial-lesson transcript, a Zoom AI summary, or your own notes, press "Fill with AI", and the text is sorted into the six sections. The teacher's job is to read what landed in each section, fix what is wrong, and press "Save".

The order matters: nothing the AI wrote is saved on its own. The teacher reviews it first. What a student said in a trial is their own words, and a teacher confirming "this is what they told me" carries more for the next person reading it than an unreviewed summary would.

Once saved, the interview stays on that student's record. When teachers change, the new one reads what the student said at the trial before the first lesson, not after.

## The first step

At your next trial lesson, ask permission and record it once. Online, a Zoom AI summary is enough. Afterwards, paste it in, press "Fill with AI", fix it, save. Under five minutes. Those five minutes turn the first month after enrollment from reconnaissance into teaching.

For the people who do not enroll, use the same screen and leave one line under "what was hard in the trial". After three months of those lines, the reason enrollment is flat starts to show.
