Japan pipeline is flat, traffic is up, and someone at headquarters decides the brand is unknown there. A localisation budget follows.

Before signing it, ask the Japan team one question. How many deals last quarter reached the buyer's shortlist and were cut from it?

Almost no Japan team can answer. That is the whole problem.

## Two different losses, hiding inside one word

In the first, the buyer never put you on the list. That is reach.

In the second, the buyer put you on the list, compared you against two or three others, and took you off. That is resolution, and impressions do nothing to it.

Merge them under "awareness" and the next decision tilts toward spend every time, because spend is the only lever the word suggests.

## In Japan, the second loss never reaches your reps

A joint survey by IDEATECH and Hiroyasu Kitagawa (published 16 April 2026, fielded 23 to 26 March 2026, n=307, limited to respondents who took part with at least one colleague in Japanese B2B purchases of 3 million yen or more; a vendor-sponsored survey skewed toward larger, multi-stakeholder deals) found that by the time serious vendor conversations begin, the buying process is on average about 40% complete.

Roughly 90% of those buyers had already shortlisted several vendors before engaging a rep, and in about 70% of cases the final comparison came down to two or three.

So the Japanese buyer who shortlisted you, evaluated you quietly, and dropped you never speaks to your rep. Nothing about them lands in your CRM.

On the headquarters dashboard, that reads as absence of demand.

## What gets you cut is size and proof, not exposure

In the same survey, the top reasons for removing a vendor were missing functionality at 39.1%, company size not being a fit at 34.5%, and no case study from a similar company at 26.4% (multiple response).

Ask the buyers who chose someone why, and the top answer is "there was a case study from a company of similar size" at 44.3%, ahead of clear functionality at 40.3% (this question only, n=298).

Neither of those moves with media spend. You would be sending more people to the same page that already lost them.

## Split the losses before you size the budget

Open last quarter's Japanese losses and non-responses, and sort them by whether the buyer named you first or you named yourself. That single test separates reach from resolution well enough to act on.

If resolution is the bigger pile, rebuild the Japanese case library by company size crossed with industry rather than by industry alone. In the same survey, industry-specific information was decisive for 16.9% of buyers and reinforced the decision for another 45.6%, so 62.5% were influenced by it. A translated wall of global enterprise logos does not answer the question a Japanese buyer is actually asking, which is whether a company shaped like theirs solved this.

If reach is the bigger pile, then fund reach, but not into last year's channels. The same survey found 91.2% of buyers used generative AI or AI search during evaluation, mostly for comparison and price research. The place where Japanese shortlists get assembled has moved. Most media plans have not.

This is the same work as getting your [Japan GTM and messaging](https://consilegy.com/en/services-en/japan-market-gtm-messaging/) right, because both the case library and the positioning have to come from Japanese deals rather than translation.

**Awareness stays a hypothesis until you have counted the deals that made the shortlist and lost it.**

## Common mistakes

- **Funding awareness because pipeline is flat.** Flat pipeline is consistent with both losses. Size the budget after you split them, not before.
- **Translating the global case studies and calling it localisation.** The buyer is checking for a company of their size in their industry. A translated US enterprise logo answers neither.
- **Reading "we had never heard of you" as evidence.** In Japan it is also a polite way to end a conversation. Weigh what the buyer did over what they said on the way out.
