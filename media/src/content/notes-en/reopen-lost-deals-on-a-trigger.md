Headquarters asks why the Japan closed-lost list is not producing win-backs. Marketing ran the standard re-engagement sequence six months after close. Nobody replied.

**The sequence assumed a deal comes back because time has passed. In Japan it comes back because something changed on the buyer's side, and nobody wrote down what that something was.**

## The closed-lost list is rarely opened, because it says nothing about when to open it

Fast Marketing Inc. surveyed 432 people at B2B companies who work in marketing or sales (online survey, May 2026, "BtoB Marketing Survey 2026"). 46.3% said that 30% or more of their prospects were going without follow-up. The figure is not limited to lost deals, but it shows how common it is for a company to have made contact with a buyer and then done nothing with it.

The rep did not neglect the account. At the moment the deal was closed, nothing in the record said what to do next or when. There is no reason to open a row like that.

## The same loss reason reopens for different reasons

Sorting loss reasons well does not tell you when to go back. We covered the design of the dropdown in [Why every lost deal in Japan shows up as "price" in your CRM](/en/articles/loss-reason-defaults-to-price/). The restart condition sits somewhere else, and it differs by reason.

- **Budget did not fit.** The trigger is the buyer's next budget request cycle, not your quarter. Many Japanese companies run a fiscal year from April, and the request is drafted months before the money exists.
- **A competitor won.** The trigger is the buyer's review of that contract. Annual contracts commonly carry a notice period before renewal, so the useful window is shortly before that deadline, not after the decision.
- **A feature was missing.** The trigger is on your side: ship it, then tell that specific buyer. Waiting six months does nothing.
- **Approval stalled, or the evaluation stopped.** The trigger is a change of people. When the champion or the approver moves, a stalled case can be raised again in a different context.

One status, "closed-lost", contains four different ways to restart. A fixed-delay nurture sequence fits at most one of them.

## The restart condition can only be written when the deal is closed

Three months later, the record says "budget". It does not say whether the buyer said "we will revisit at the start of next fiscal year" or "this is not our department's call". Only the rep who held the deal knows.

So the record is made in the same action that closes the deal. Three fields:

1. What would need to change for this to restart: budget cycle, competitor renewal, a feature, a personnel change.
2. The date to check, even if it is only a month.
3. Who checks.

Make "not applicable" a valid answer for the first field. If every field is mandatory, reps type something to get past the screen and the data gets worse. We wrote about that failure in [In Japan, a required field produces confident wrong answers](/en/articles/dont-make-unknown-fields-required/).

One more number from the same survey. Where results of handed-off leads were shared back down to the reason for the win or loss, 82.1% reported meeting their sales target. Where sharing stopped at whether someone had followed up, it was 54.3%. The report itself states that this is a correlation, not causation, and the publisher sells marketing strategy support, so its conclusion favours its own business. Read the percentages as direction and rough size.

The direction is clear enough. When the reason and the restart condition reach marketing, marketing can write to that buyer about that condition. When they do not, the only option is the same email to everyone.

## Automate the reminder, not the message

The tempting design is to send different automated emails per loss reason. That works up to a point: a buyer who left on cost can reasonably receive cost-saving case studies. Beyond that it backfires. A buyer whose condition is "the competitor's renewal" gets nothing from a periodic email.

Automate only this: on the check date, notify the rep. When the notification arrives, the previous restart condition should be visible on the screen. The rep then checks one thing about the buyer's situation, by whatever channel suits the relationship.

Decide who enters the condition. It is whoever presses the closed-lost button. A field without a named owner is empty within six months.

## What the Japan team can do this week

Open the last ten closed-lost deals from the past quarter and count how many have a restart condition and a check date. If the answer is zero, the closed-lost list is a graveyard, not an inventory.

Then ask the Japan lead to add the three fields to the close screen, with "not applicable" allowed on the first. HQ marketing should hold off on any win-back campaign for Japan until the first month of entries comes in.
